What to Do During a Plumbing Emergency: A Step-by-Step Guide

Burst pipe? Overflowing toilet? Gas leak? Here's exactly what to do in the first 10 minutes of a plumbing emergency to minimize damage and stay safe.

Robert Hayes
Senior Plumber
1 January 2026
3 min read

A plumbing emergency can happen at any time — and when it does, every minute counts. Whether it's a burst pipe flooding your living room or an overflowing toilet ruining your bathroom floor, knowing exactly what to do in the first few minutes can mean the difference between a minor inconvenience and a major disaster. Here's your step-by-step guide.

1. Stay calm and assess the situation

Panicking leads to poor decisions. Take a deep breath and quickly assess what's happening. Is water actively flooding? Is there a gas smell? Is the water hot or cold? Understanding the nature of the emergency helps you take the right action and communicate clearly when you call for help.

2. Shut off the water immediately

The single most important step in any water-related plumbing emergency is stopping the flow. Know where your main water shutoff valve is — it's usually near the water meter or where the main line enters your home. Turn it clockwise to close it. If the problem is isolated to one fixture (like a toilet or sink), use the local shutoff valve near that fixture instead.

3. Turn off electricity if needed

If water is flooding near electrical outlets, appliances, or your breaker panel, turn off the electricity at the main breaker. Water and electricity are a deadly combination. Never wade into standing water if any electrical source may be active. If you're unsure, always err on the side of caution and kill the power.

4. What to do if you smell gas

If you smell gas, do NOT turn on any switches, light matches, or use your phone inside the house. Evacuate everyone immediately, leave the door open, and call your gas company's emergency line from outside. A gas leak is a life-threatening emergency that requires immediate professional response. Do not re-enter the home until the gas company confirms it's safe.

5. Contain the damage

While waiting for the plumber, minimize damage by moving furniture, electronics, and valuables away from the water. Use towels, buckets, and mops to contain the spread. If water is coming through the ceiling, place buckets below the leak and carefully puncture a small hole in the bulging drywall to let water drain — this prevents the ceiling from collapsing under the weight.

6. Call a professional plumber

Once you've stopped the water and contained the damage, call a licensed emergency plumber. Describe the situation clearly: what happened, what you've done so far, and whether electricity or gas is involved. A professional can guide you over the phone while dispatching a technician. Keep your plumber's emergency number saved in your phone before you need it.
