Bathroom Renovation: What You Need to Know Before You Start

A complete guide to planning your bathroom renovation — from budgeting and design to plumbing upgrades and choosing the right fixtures.

Jennifer Lee
Bathroom Design Specialist
2 January 2026
8 min read

A bathroom renovation is one of the most rewarding home improvement projects you can undertake. It improves your daily quality of life and offers one of the highest returns on investment of any renovation — typically recouping 60-70% of costs in increased home value. But without proper planning, a bathroom remodel can quickly become stressful and expensive. Here's everything you need to know before you start.

1. Set a realistic budget

Bathroom renovations in Hyderabad typically range from ₹50,000 for a basic refresh to ₹3,00,000+ for a luxury remodel. Allocate your budget roughly as: 40% fixtures and materials, 30% labor, 15% plumbing and electrical, 10% tiling and waterproofing, and 5% contingency for unexpected issues. Always keep a 10-15% buffer for surprises — once walls are opened, hidden problems often surface.

2. Plan your layout carefully

The most expensive part of a bathroom renovation is moving plumbing. If you keep your toilet, shower, and sink in their existing locations, you'll save significantly. If you must change the layout, work with a plumber early in the design phase to understand what's feasible and cost-effective. Consider traffic flow, door swing clearance, and storage needs when finalizing your layout.

3. Upgrade your plumbing while walls are open

During a renovation, your walls will be open — this is the ideal time to replace old or corroded pipes. Upgrading from galvanized steel to modern PEX or copper piping prevents future leaks and improves water pressure. Also consider installing a dedicated shutoff valve for the bathroom, making future maintenance much easier.

4. Don't skimp on waterproofing

Waterproofing is the single most critical step in a bathroom renovation. Improper waterproofing leads to mold, structural damage, and costly repairs down the line. Ensure your contractor applies waterproof membrane to all shower walls, floors, and wet areas according to local building codes. This is not the place to cut corners — the cost of redoing waterproofing after tiles are installed is enormous.

5. Choose quality fixtures

Invest in quality fixtures that will last. Look for taps and showerheads with ceramic disc cartridges — they last longer and drip less than traditional washer-based fittings. Choose water-efficient models with WaterSense ratings. For toilets, consider dual-flush models that use 4-6 liters per flush versus older models that use 12+ liters. Quality fixtures cost more upfront but save on water bills and repairs for years.

6. Install proper ventilation

A good exhaust fan is essential for preventing mold and moisture damage. Choose a fan rated for your bathroom's square footage, and ensure it vents outside — not into the attic or wall cavity. Consider a humidity-sensing fan that turns on automatically when moisture levels rise. Proper ventilation extends the life of your paint, grout, and fixtures significantly.

7. Expect the timeline to stretch

A typical bathroom renovation takes 2-3 weeks, but delays are common. Fixtures may be backordered, hidden water damage may require additional repairs, or tiling may take longer than expected. Plan for the bathroom to be unusable for at least 2 weeks and have a backup plan for showering and toileting. Good communication with your contractor about the timeline helps manage expectations.

Pro tip

Always hire a licensed plumber for bathroom renovations. DIY plumbing work often fails inspections, voids home insurance, and can cause thousands in water damage. A professional ensures everything is done to code and backed by a warranty.
