10 Ways to Prevent Clogged Drains This Winter

Winter brings unique challenges for your plumbing. Here are 10 proven ways to keep your drains flowing freely all season long.

Marcus Thompson
Drain Cleaning Expert
8 January 2026
4 min read

Winter is tough on your plumbing. Cold weather, holiday cooking, and increased indoor activity all put extra strain on your drains. A clogged drain is more than just an inconvenience — it can lead to water damage, foul odors, and costly repairs. The good news? Most clogs are entirely preventable with a few simple habits.

1. Never pour grease down the drain

Cooking grease and oil may be liquid when warm, but they solidify as they cool inside your pipes. This creates a sticky buildup that catches food particles and gradually blocks the drain entirely. Instead, pour grease into a container, let it cool, and throw it in the trash.

2. Use drain screens

Drain screens are inexpensive and incredibly effective. Place them over your kitchen and shower drains to catch food scraps, hair, and soap scum before they enter your pipes. Clean the screens regularly to keep water flowing freely.

3. Run hot water regularly

Once a week, run hot water down each drain for 30 seconds. This helps dissolve any buildup of soap, grease, or oil before it becomes a blockage. For kitchen sinks, boiling water is even more effective at cutting through grease.

4. Baking soda and vinegar flush

Once a month, pour half a cup of baking soda down the drain, followed by half a cup of white vinegar. Cover the drain for 15 minutes, then flush with hot water. This natural combination breaks down buildup and keeps your drains fresh without harsh chemicals.

5. Keep pipes warm in cold weather

In winter, pipes in exterior walls or unheated areas can freeze, leading to blockages and even burst pipes. Insulate exposed pipes with foam sleeves, and open cabinet doors under sinks to let warm air circulate. On extremely cold nights, let a faucet drip slightly to keep water moving.

6. Be careful with your garbage disposal

Your garbage disposal isn't a trash can. Avoid putting fibrous foods (celery, onion skins), starchy foods (potato peels, pasta), or hard items (bones, fruit pits) down the disposal. Always run cold water while the disposal is running, and keep it running for a few seconds after the food is gone to flush the pipes.

7. Manage hair in shower drains

Hair is the number one cause of shower drain clogs. A mesh hair catcher costs just a few rupees and saves you from expensive plumbing bills. Clean it after every shower. If you notice slow draining, address it immediately before it becomes a full blockage.

8. Avoid harsh chemical drain cleaners

Store-bought chemical drain cleaners may temporarily clear a clog, but they corrode your pipes over time and can cause more damage than the original blockage. Stick to natural methods like baking soda and vinegar, or call a professional for stubborn clogs.

9. Schedule annual drain inspection

A professional camera inspection can identify potential problems — tree root intrusion, pipe scale buildup, or partial blockages — before they cause a backup. Annual inspections are especially important for older homes where pipes may be deteriorating.

10. Consider preventive hydro-jetting

For homes with frequent drain issues, preventive hydro-jetting every 1-2 years can keep pipes thoroughly clean. This high-pressure water treatment removes all buildup from pipe walls, restoring them to near-original condition. It's a cost-effective way to avoid emergency plumbing calls.

Key takeaway

90% of drain clogs are preventable. A few minutes of maintenance each week can save you thousands in emergency plumbing repairs this winter.
